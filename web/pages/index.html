§ 02 / Structural Fragility

Asymmetric security collapses when algebraic preconditions fail.

Fundamental Congruence
me·d ≡ m1 + k·φ(N) ≡ m (mod N)

Security relies strictly on the computational intractability of deriving φ(N) or d from (N, e). Any structural bias in p, q, or e leaks algebraic relations.

In textbook cryptography, RSA appears monolithic: choose two large primes, multiply them into a modulus N, and publish an exponent e. Yet in deployed systems, cryptographic failure rarely stems from brute-force factoring of well-formed integers.

Instead, vulnerabilities emerge from subtle number-theoretic degeneracies: primes generated with insufficient entropy, moduli sharing common factors across devices, private exponents chosen too small for fast decryption, or unpadded messages encrypted under low exponents.

Invariant I
Prime Separation
|p − q| must exceed 2N1/4 to prevent Fermat's difference-of-squares factorization.
Invariant II
Exponent Magnitude
Private key d must satisfy d > N0.292 to resist continued-fraction and lattice attacks.
Invariant III
Randomized Padding
Plaintext m must be randomized via OAEP so me wraps modulo N multiple times.

§ 05 / Taxonomy of Cryptanalytic Attacks

Classical & Algebraic Attack Families

Six fundamental vectors analyzed within the RSA-Lab engine.

Class I · Prime Proximity O(|p − q|²)

Fermat Factorization

N = a² − b² = (a − b)(a + b)

When two primes p and q lie close together, their arithmetic mean a = (p + q)/2 sits just above √N. Incremental search reveals b² = a² − N as a perfect square.

Class II · Small Private Key d < ⅓ N1/4

Wiener's Attack

| e/N − k/d | < 1 / (2d²)

To accelerate decryption, implementations occasionally select a small private exponent d. By Legendre's theorem on Diophantine approximations, k/d appears among the continued-fraction convergents of e/N.

Class III · Un-wrapped Modulus me < N

Low Exponent Root

c ≡ m³ (mod N) → m = ∛c

When e = 3 is used without randomized padding on a short message, m³ never exceeds the modulus N. Modular reduction never occurs, reducing decryption to an exact integer cube root.

Class IV · Entropy Collision O(log² N)

Shared Prime GCD

gcd(N₁, N₂) = p > 1

When hardware PRNGs initialize with low entropy at boot, distinct devices generate moduli sharing a common prime factor p. Euclid's algorithm extracts p instantaneously without factoring either modulus.

Class V · B-smooth Order p − 1 | B!

Pollard's p − 1

gcd(aB! − 1, N) = p

If p − 1 consists exclusively of small prime powers, Fermat's little theorem guarantees aB! ≡ 1 (mod p), exposing p via modular exponentiation.

Class VI · Bézout Identity gcd(e₁, e₂) = 1

Common Modulus

c₁a · c₂b ≡ m (mod N)

Encrypting the identical plaintext m under the same modulus N with coprime exponents e₁, e₂ permits recovery of m via the extended Euclidean algorithm without factoring N.

§ 06 / Constructive Defense

Hardened Key Generation

Defeating classical and lattice cryptanalysis requires enforcing strict algebraic boundaries at the moment of prime synthesis — before a single bit is published.

Modulus bit-length
|N| ≥ 3072 bits (128-bit security)
Public exponent
e = 65537 (2¹⁶ + 1)
Padding scheme
RSA-OAEP (SHA-256) / RSA-PSS
Primality certification
Miller–Rabin (64 rounds) + Lucas
01 / Entropy & CSPRNG

Independent Prime Sampling

Primes p and q are drawn independently from a hardware-seeded CSPRNG after entropy-pool health verification, eliminating shared-factor collisions across distributed fleets.

02 / Proximity Guard

Fermat Separation Bound

Candidate primes are rejected whenever |p − q| ≤ 2n/2 − 100, guaranteeing that the arithmetic mean (p + q)/2 remains exponentially distant from √N.

03 / Exponent Floor

Carmichael Totient λ(N)

Private exponent d is computed modulo λ(N) = lcm(p − 1, q − 1) and verified against the Boneh–Durfee lattice bound d > N0.292.

04 / Constant-Time CRT

Blinded Modular Arithmetic

Decryption employs Montgomery-ladder exponentiation with random multiplicative blinding re · c (mod N) to neutralize timing and fault-injection side channels.
